Road Corridor

Masterplan Nickname: Corridor

Overview

Sweeps a road cross-section template along a 3D centreline (e.g. from Auto-Grade Road), daylights cut and fill slopes to the terrain, and reports earthworks to subgrade, pavement quantities and a mass haul.

Inputs (10)

  • Centreline (Curve): 3D road centreline. Its Z is the finished road level on the axis (use Auto-Grade Road for a draped, slope-limited profile).
  • Terrain (Mesh): Existing ground mesh.
  • Template (Text): Preset name (residential street, urban street, rural road, cycle path, access track), a JSON template, or Road Network's Corridor Templates (one per Corridor Edge) so the 3D matches the 2D plan.
  • Interval (Number): Distance between cross sections (m).
  • Design Speed (Number): Design speed (km/h), used for superelevation on curves.
  • Max Superelevation (Number): Maximum superelevation (%). 0 = keep the normal crown (typical for urban streets).
  • Runoff Length (Number): Length (m) over which superelevation is introduced.
  • Cut Reuse Factor (Number): Share of bank cut that can be reused as compacted fill (0 to 1).
  • Free Haul (Number): Free-haul distance (m) for the overhaul figure.
  • Run (Boolean): False = keep showing the last results (cached).

Outputs (14)

  • Design Surface (Mesh): Finished road surface with daylight slopes, coloured by part.
  • Subgrade (Mesh): Underside of the pavement build-up (formation level for earthworks).
  • Feature Lines (Curve): 3D lines along every part edge.
  • Daylight Lines (Curve): Where the cut / fill slopes meet the ground, left and right.
  • Sections (Curve): Design cross section at each station (3D).
  • Ground Sections (Curve): Existing ground along each cross section (3D).
  • Stations (Number): Chainage of each cross section (m).
  • Cut Areas (Number): Cut area to subgrade per section (m²).
  • Fill Areas (Number): Fill area to subgrade per section (m²).
  • Mass Haul (Curve): Mass haul diagram drawn below the corridor: mass line and balance (zero) line. X = chainage, Y = scaled volume.
  • Quantities (Text): JSON: earthworks, mass haul, pavement layer volumes, surface areas, kerb length.
  • Template (Text): The template used, as editable JSON.
  • Info (Text): Method, assumptions, simplifications and references.
  • Dashboard Data (Text): JSON legend and metrics (for Analysis Dashboard and Report Metrics).

Method, assumptions and simplifications

The same notes appear in the component's Info output.

Method

  1. Cross sections every Interval along the centreline (and at its end), square to its plan direction.
  2. Each side of the template is built outward from the axis: an optional rise (kerb upstand), then the part width at its cross slope (negative % falls away from the axis).
  3. Superelevation on curves: e = min(eMax, V² / 127 R) %, applied to lane parts (outside lane up to +e, inside to -max(crown, e)), smoothed with a moving average over the Runoff Length.
  4. Daylight: from the outer edge a cut slope rises (or a fill slope falls) at 1 : H until it meets the terrain, found by 0.25 m steps then bisection, up to the template's maximum daylight width.
  5. Earthwork line: the subgrade (surface minus the pavement layer depth) under paved parts, the finished surface elsewhere. Cut and fill areas integrate the gap to the existing ground every 0.1 m across the section.
  6. Volumes by average end area between sections; pavement layer volumes and surface areas the same way.
  7. Mass haul: cumulative (cut × Cut Reuse Factor - fill) along the road; balance points where it crosses zero; haul = area between the mass line and zero (m³ × m); average haul = haul / volume moved; volume beyond free haul = loop height above the level where the loop is Free Haul wide.

Assumptions

  • The centreline Z is the finished road level on the axis (use Auto-Grade Road for a draped, slope-limited profile).
  • Daylight slopes are given as H:V (2 = 1 vertical in 2 horizontal).
  • Cut Reuse Factor turns bank cut into usable compacted fill (losses and shrinkage together).
  • Preset templates use typical dimensions for early design, not a specific standard: check them against the local street design guide.
  • Ground is read by vertical rays onto the terrain mesh; sections off the terrain or beyond the maximum daylight are flagged (partial volumes).

Simplifications and limits

  • Superelevation ignores side friction (e = V² / 127 R, capped at eMax), which overstates it on gentle curves.
  • No prismoidal or curvature correction to average end area; on tight curves the inner sides of sections can overlap and volumes are overstated.
  • Topsoil strip and bulking are not separated; earthwork under kerbs uses the kerb bed depth.
  • Conditional parts (ditches only in cut) are tested at the part's start.
  • Junctions are not handled here: feed Road Network's Corridor Edges so corridors stop at the junction mouths.

Workflow

  • Road Network -> Corridor Edges -> Auto-Grade Road -> Centreline; Corridor Templates -> Template; terrain -> Terrain.
  • Quantities and Dashboard Data -> Report Metrics; Mass Haul curves are drawn below the corridor.

References

  • AASHTO, A Policy on Geometric Design of Highways and Streets (Green Book): e + f = V² / 127 R.
  • Average end area method and mass haul (Brückner) diagram: standard earthworks practice.
